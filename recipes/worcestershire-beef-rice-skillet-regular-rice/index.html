Savory Worcestershire Beef and Rice Skillet (Regular Rice)

A savory, comforting skillet with ground beef, uncooked white rice, peas and carrots, and a lightly thickened Worcestershire beef-broth sauce.

4 generous portions40–45 minutes

Ingredients

  • 1 lb lean ground beef
  • 1 cup uncooked long-grain white rice
  • 1 steamable package of peas and carrots (10–12 oz)
  • 2½ cups beef broth
  • 1–2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, to taste and tolerance
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1½ tbsp cornstarch
  • ½ tsp dried parsley
  • ¼ tsp dried thyme
  • ¼ tsp onion powder (optional, if tolerated)
  • Salt to taste
  • Up to ½ cup additional water or broth, as needed

Instructions

  1. Cook the vegetables: Microwave the steamable peas and carrots according to package directions. Set aside.
  2. Brown the beef: In a large, deep skillet with a lid, brown the ground beef over medium heat, breaking it into small pieces. Drain excess grease.
  3. Add seasonings: Stir in the Worcestershire sauce, butter, parsley, thyme, optional onion powder, and a little salt.
  4. Add rice and broth: Stir in the uncooked rice and beef broth. Bring everything to a gentle boil.
  5. Cook the rice: Reduce heat to low, cover tightly, and simmer for approximately 18–20 minutes, or until the rice is tender and most of the liquid has been absorbed. Follow the rice package's timing and avoid lifting the lid frequently.
  6. Add vegetables: Stir in the cooked peas and carrots.
  7. Thicken the sauce: Mix the cornstarch with 2 tablespoons cold water until smooth. Stir into the skillet and simmer uncovered for 2–3 minutes, until the sauce thickens.
  8. Adjust consistency: Add up to ½ cup additional broth or water if needed. If the rice is still firm, add ¼ cup hot water, cover, and continue cooking for another 3–5 minutes. Taste and adjust salt and Worcestershire sauce.

For reflux sensitivity: Worcestershire sauce contains vinegar and may aggravate symptoms for some people. Start with 1 tablespoon or omit it if it is a known trigger. Check the label for onion and garlic, too.

Rice note: This version is written for ordinary long-grain white rice. Brown rice needs a different amount of liquid and a longer cooking time.
